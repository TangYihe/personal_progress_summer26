Naming is **J1–J6, no J0. J1 is at the BOTTOM (base), J6 at the TOP** (where arms + head attach).
Config arrays are index 0 = J1 (`start_degrees`, `rest_joint_weights`, `smoothness_joint_weights`,
`operating_limit_margin_degrees` all share this layout).

| Joint | Height | Axis | Analogy | Start | Effective range |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| J1 | 0.100 m | roll | base roll | 0° | ±10.5° |
| J2 | 0.265 m | **pitch** | ankle | +60° | ±89.5° |
| J3 | 0.415 m | **pitch** | knee | −70° | −139.5…+59.5° |

  fingers point along **+x** = the robot's front. (`embodiments.yaml`'s `-y -> front` comment is in

**🔑 Why the torso felt "stiff" — it is not a lock, and not stiffness.** The IK has **no torso
objective at all**: targets are only `left_wrist`/`right_wrist` (`head: hold` → no headset target).
Nothing ever *asks* the torso to move. The only force on it is the rest regularizer pulling toward
the start pose, which is vertical, at weight 12.0 vs the arms' 4.0. So the solver reaches with the
arms and leaves the legs alone. **The rest weights are the whole story** — velocity caps and
smoothing were the wrong first lever.

**Weights tried (`ik.rest_joint_weights.torso`, all from a 12.0 baseline):**
| Setting | Result |
|---|---|
| `[12,12,12,6,6,12]` (J4 waist + J5 roll) | "Indeed better, a lot more leaning" |
| `[12,12,12,6,12,6]` (J4 + J6 yaw) | J5→J6 swap; roll not the useful DOF |
| `[12,8,8,8,12,6]` (whole pitch chain) | pitch distributed across ankle/knee/waist |
| **`[12,5,5,8,12,6]`** ← **KEEPER** | **squats properly; verified sim + robot w/ hands** |
| `[12,3,3,4,12,6]` (pitch halved again) | **TOO SHAKY on the robot — reverted** |

**The 5/5/8 row is the settled value.** Going to 3/3/4 was tested on hardware and is too shaky, so
the useful range on the pitch chain is narrow — roughly 5–8, not 3. Below ~5 the stance anchor also
gets too weak to hold `start_degrees` (see the rest-reference note below).

Plus `smoothness_joint_weights.torso` **3.0 → 6.0** across all six: with the legs carrying the whole
upper body, tick-to-tick variation is far more visible than when the torso was held.

**✅ RESOLVED — weight tuning was the wrong lever; stances are the answer.** The first worry was that
squat-tuned weights would make the robot stand up too much when the arms raise. On test both cases
were acceptable at 5/5/8, and the deeper insight is that **weights only change the IK's *preference* —
the arms are always cheaper than moving the whole body, so no weight set makes the robot choose a big
posture change.** Reaching high or low is solved by **changing the stance** (`start_degrees`), not by
loosening weights. See the stance gotcha below. Weights stay at the one calm value, 5/5/8.

**⚠️ THERE IS NO COLLISION CHECKING ANYWHERE IN THIS CODE** (grepped `src/teleop/control/`,
`ik_process.py` — nothing). Nothing stops the hand from hitting the chassis. Matters now that the
torso moves. What actually exists: joint limits, the coupled wrist envelope, driver-side validation
and rate shaping, `max_joint_step_rad: 1.0`.
- **The "teleop gets stuck near the chassis" feel is the IK residual gate, not a safety system** —

  `max_position_error_m` (50 mm) the solve is rejected; with a joint pinned on a limit it is
  tolerated to 2× (100 mm) and reported as a saturation shortfall; past that it hard-fails → the
  tick goes to **HOLD**, the arm stops dead and re-engages over the full `action_engagement_ms:
  1000` ramp. It fires when the arm **can't follow you**, which is *not* chassis protection.
- **Reach bound (torso held):** lowest right palm ≈ **48.6 cm** above the base plane — roughly knee
  height. **Ground reach is impossible with `torso: hold`.** (The torso-free number from the same
  search was non-physical — no floor/collision model, so it swung the body below the base plane.
  Kinematic bounds only; the chassis will physically block part of the envelope.)

**🔎 `head: pico` — TESTED on the robot 08-04, WORKS but too shaky → reverted to `head: hold`.**
It adds the headset as a third IK target and puts Head_J1/J2 in the active set (22 joints).
Head_J1 is pitch ±30°, Head_J2 is yaw ±90°, and **both are pure rotations that cannot translate the
head**, so a headset *position* target can only be satisfied by moving the legs — lean your body and
the robot bends, i.e. genuinely *commanded* lean rather than emergent reaching.
- ⚠️ **Why it was reverted: headset tracking noise propagates into the whole upper body.** With
  `torso: hold` the held torso absorbs that noise; with the torso in the IK set it becomes visible
  motion. Directly against the anti-shake goal. (The old PICO VIO-snap class of fault would now move
  the torso too, not just an arm.) Also three targets — headset + both wrists — compete for the same
  leg joints in one weighted solve, so the wrists win less often.
- ⚠️ Tested *together with* the 3/3/4 weights, so the two are not fully separated — but both push the
  same way and 5/5/8 + `head: hold` is the verified-calm combination.

**🎬 Mechanism choice for the shoot — worth a decision.** Under `torso: ik` there is **no squat
command**: the legs only move as redundant DOF serving the wrist targets, so the squat is *emergent*
from where the operator's hands are and **will not repeat take to take**. A captured init pose ramped
via `Tab` is repeatable and directable, and the reset ramp already runs a visibly smooth
`0.2` rad/s / `0.4` rad/s² (`config/runtime.yaml:58-59`). **For a movement-heavy script, scripted
pose-to-pose ramps may simply beat teleoped IK.**
- Init poses are pure data — a flat 22-value joint vector (`config/init_poses/<name>.yaml`, torso 6 +
  head 2 + left arm 7 + right arm 7), so candidates can be authored and checked offline with FK, no
  robot and no operator.
- **Squat depth available with the torso kept exactly vertical** (proportional J2/J3/J4 scaling holds
  tilt at 0.00°): **16.4 cm** drop for ≤2 cm forward shift · 21.1 cm for ≤10 cm · 25.0 cm for ≤15 cm.
  **J2's 89.5° limit binds** in every shallow-shift solution. The unconstrained maximum (28.6 cm)
  throws the upper body **+34 cm forward**, which is a tipping question over the Luna base that
  kinematics can't answer — avoid.

**🔑 THE STANCE GOTCHA — `start_degrees` IS the IK rest reference.** An `init_pose` alone does **not**
change the stance teleop holds. `rest_joint_positions = robot.start_joint_positions`
([model.py:189](../../../we-teleop/src/teleop/robots/tianji/model.py#L189)), bound once at solver
construction and never linked to the active init pose. So `init_pose stance_low` + `Tab` moves the
robot there, but the moment teleop goes live the rest term drags the torso back toward `start_degrees`
while the arms stretch to keep the wrists on target. **To change stance you must edit
`start_degrees.torso`.**
- ⚠️ **This one DOES need the Orin sync** (unlike the IK weights, which are operator-side only): the
  driver reads `start_joint_positions` for `move-to-init` and the convergence gate
  ([driver_supervisor.py:513](../../../we-teleop/src/teleop/runtime/driver_supervisor.py#L513),
  [driver.py:654](../../../we-teleop/src/teleop/robots/tianji/driver.py#L654)). Hosts disagreeing =
  driver moves to one stance while the IK rests at another → permanent fight + wrong gate target.
- ⚠️ **No runtime stance switch.** Global to the embodiment, read once per process, needed on both
  hosts → switching = edit + teleop restart + Orin sync. Workable per-scene, **not per-take**, and it
  **cannot coexist with an autonomous shot at the trained stance** (the validated 16/16 policy was
  trained at `[0,60,-70,10,0,0]`; deploy and replay both key off `start_degrees`).
- **Three stances now live in a comment block in `embodiments.yaml` — swap the active line:**
  | Stance | `torso` | Shoulder | vs original | Fwd shift | Head |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | ORIGINAL | `[0, 60, -70, 10, 0, 0]` | 0.710 m | — | — | 1.209 m |
  | HIGH | `[0, 19.0, -0.5, -18.5, 0, 0]` | 0.833 m | **+12.3 cm** | +1.5 cm | 1.332 m |
  | LOW | `[0, 81.5, -118.0, 36.5, 0, 0]` | 0.551 m | **−16.0 cm** | +8.9 cm | 1.049 m |
- All three keep **J2+J3+J4 = 0** → upper body exactly vertical (verified through the real loader,
  no limit violations). **HIGH needs J4 *negative* (−18.5°)** to cancel the forward swing from
  straightening the knee — that is what keeps the body over the base while gaining 12 cm.
- ⚠️ **LOW has only 8° of J2 headroom** (81.5° vs the 89.5° operating limit). Since `start_degrees` is
  also the rest reference, that is all the further squat the IK has from that stance — and a pinned
  joint feeds the saturation/HOLD path. Back J2 off ~5° for ~2 cm if it stalls.
- Both verified in sim **and on the robot with gloves**; `stance_high`/`stance_low` also exist as

**💪 ARM COMPLIANCE — how to make the arms softer. Tested on the robot 08-04, confirmed
satisfactory, then reverted to default. Travelling at DEFAULT.**

> **To soften the arms: `gento.arm_pd_kp: [6.0, 6.0, 6.0, 6.0, 1.5, 1.5, 1.5]`** (proximal J1–J4
> only, 9.0 → 6.0). Leave `arm_pd_kd` alone. Then **Orin sync + driver restart**.
> Default is `[9.0, 9.0, 9.0, 9.0, 1.5, 1.5, 1.5]`; both are recorded in a comment on the line.

- **`kp` is stiffness — lower = softer. `kd` is damping, NOT softness** — lowering `kd` adds
  oscillation rather than compliance, so it stays untouched. Softening `kp` alone actually *raises*
  the damping ratio, so it goes soft and calm together.
- **The wrists are already soft** (`1.5` vs `9.0` proximal), so "softer arm" means the proximal `9.0`.
  The torso is far stiffer (`torso_pd_kp: [40,40,40,18,18,12]`) — a separate lever, newly relevant now
  that the torso moves.
- **The parameter map** (all `embodiments.yaml` → `gento:`, all consumed driver-side in
  [gento.py:385-420](../../../we-teleop/src/teleop/robots/tianji/gento.py#L385-L420)):
  `arm_pd_kp`/`arm_pd_kd` under `--control-mode pd` (default) · `arm_impedance_kp`/`arm_impedance_kd`
  under `--control-mode impedance` · `torso_pd_kp`/`torso_pd_kd` always.
- ⚠️ **These are DRIVER-side** → every gain experiment costs an Orin sync + driver restart. The 07-03
  `PD_ARM_KP`/`PD_ARM_KD` env-var overrides are **gone from this tree**, so there is no fast-iteration
  path any more (on the upstream-report list).
- ⚠️ **`operating_limit_margin_degrees` was sized from tracking error measured at `kp: 9.0`** — wrist
  entries are 3.0° from a measured p99.9 of 3.22°, and the config states the margin must cover servo
  tracking error "or a command at the operating limit lands the joint on the mechanical stop."
  **Softer gains under-size those margins** → do not drive softened joints to their operating limits.
- ⚠️ Softer gains also mean **more droop** (1.1 kg per hand, no brake on the arms), **larger
  steady-state error → the init gate trips more readily** (record the error before bumping it; the
  right-wrist trend is being tracked), and a **train/deploy mismatch** for the validated policy, which
  was trained at `kp: 9.0`. These are the 07-03 team-tuned values that "largely mitigated" the arm
  shaking — tell the owner before any lasting change.
- **ℹ️ Untried cheaper alternative: `--control-mode impedance`** — real SDK joint impedance
  (`switch_to_imp_joint_mode`, no velocity feedforward), the architecturally compliant path, and it is
  a **driver flag only: no config edit, no Orin sync**. Caveat: its gains are flat `10`, so the wrists
  get *stiffer* (1.5 → 10) — a different feel, not simply softer. Worth one run on a day with room.
